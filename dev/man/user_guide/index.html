Concepts and Conventions

BondGraphToolkit follows the conventions of the bond-graph literature, mainly Karnopp, Margolis & Rosenberg, System Dynamics (5th ed., Wiley 2012), and Borutzky (ed.), Bond Graph Modelling of Engineering Systems (Springer 2011). This page states them once; every element and junction uses them.

Bonds and power

A bond carries one effort e and one flow f, and its half-arrow marks the positive direction of power: when e·f > 0, power flows along the arrow (Karnopp §2.2). Every element exchanges power through a PowerPort with the variables e and f, and its bond to the rest of the model is an ordinary ModelingToolkit connect.

The direction of a bond is in its ports. Every element has two: power, through which its bond points into the element, and pout, through which it points out of it. It connects one of them. A bond is a connect from an outgoing port (an element's pout, a two-port's p2, a junction's outgoing free port) to an incoming one (an element's power, a two-port's p1, a junction's incoming free port):

connect(se.pout, tf.p1)      # Se ⇀ TF
connect(tf.p2, mass.power)   # TF ⇀ I

Every element also has the variables e and f, its bond's effort and flow along the half-arrow, whichever port carries the bond; read results from them (mass.f, se.e). A port's own f is ModelingToolkit's, counted into the element, so se.pout.f is minus the bond flow.

Elements

An element's law is written in the variables of its bond as drawn (Karnopp §3.1):

ElementLawConventional drawing
Se(E)e = Ebond points away from the source
Sf(F)f = Fbond points away from the source
Resistance(R)e = R fbond points into the element
Compliance(C, q)q̇ = f, e = q / Cbond points into the element
Inertia(I, p)ṗ = e, f = p / Ibond points into the element

The tool never reinterprets a drawing: a resistance whose bond points away from it keeps e = R f in that bond's variables, so it delivers power, a negative resistor. The books don't say what a reversed one-port means; this is the package's choice, so that you always get what you drew.

Mass(m, u), Spring(k, x) and Damper(c) are mechanical shorthands for Inertia, Compliance and Resistance.

Junctions

At a 0-junction all bonds share one effort and the flows sum to zero; at a 1-junction all bonds share one flow and the efforts sum to zero. In the sum, a bond pointing into the junction counts + and one pointing out counts -. The shared variable itself is never signed (Karnopp Eqs. 3.16–3.21).

A junction member says which way its bond points:

@named m = Junction1(se => :pout, R, C)   # Se ⇀ 1, 1 ⇀ R, 1 ⇀ C

A bare element x is a bond from the junction into x (through x.power), and x => :pout a bond from x into the junction. A Symbol creates a free port for bonds to other junctions and two-ports: :out is a bond leaving the junction and :in => :in one entering it, so :out on one junction and :in => :in on another, joined with connect(j1.out, j2.in), is a bond from the first junction to the second. A connect of more than two ports is itself a 0-junction.

Two-ports

TF and GY have port p1 (bond in) and p2 (bond out): TF: e₁ = m e₂, f₂ = m f₁; GY: e₁ = r f₂, e₂ = r f₁ (Karnopp Eqs. 3.9, 3.11). A modulus that is an expression, or that comes from a signal, makes them modulated (MTF, MGY).

A source bonds to port 1 through its outgoing port, connect(se.pout, tf.p1), and port 2 to an element through the element's incoming port, connect(tf.p2, mass.power). Connecting two incoming ports, such as connect(se.power, tf.p1), is not a bond but a 0-junction with both bonds pointing out of it, which flips the sign of the source's flow; bgplot draws it that way. An element bonded through both of its ports is an error in BondGraph(model) and bgplot: a one-port has one bond.

Simulation and causality

A bond-graph model is an ordinary ModelingToolkit system: mtkcompile reduces it, and ODEProblem and solve simulate it. BondGraphToolkit doesn't assign causal strokes. ModelingToolkit chooses the states and reduces the index itself, which covers what causality analysis would tell you about the equations.

Derivative causality. Two inertias on one 1-junction share one flow, so only one of them can keep an independent momentum: in bond-graph terms, the other is in derivative causality (Karnopp §3.4). MTK keeps one momentum as a state and adds a "dummy derivative" algebraic variable for the other:

using BondGraphToolkit
@named se = Se(1.0)
@named m1 = Inertia(I = 1.0)
@named m2 = Inertia(I = 2.0, p = nothing)   # in derivative causality: no initial value
@named model = Junction1(se => :pout, m1, m2)
sys = mtkcompile(model)
unknowns(sys)
2-element Vector{SymbolicUtils.BasicSymbolicImpl.var"typeof(BasicSymbolicImpl)"{SymReal}}:
 m2₊p(t)
 core₊p2₊e(t)

The result is a small DAE (a mass-matrix ODE), so solve it with a DAE-capable solver such as Rodas5P from OrdinaryDiffEqRosenbrock rather than Tsit5.

Two things follow for elements in derivative causality:

  • No initial value. Their state is fixed by the others, so give it none: Inertia(I = 2.0, p = nothing), Compliance(C = 1.0, q = nothing). An initial value over-determines the initialisation, and MTK warns.
  • Which state MTK keeps. MTK chooses the states once, at compile time, and its choice can be singular somewhere. In the motor-driven pendulum, keeping the point mass's momentum $p_x$ recovers $\omega$ from $p_x = -m l\sin\varphi\,\omega$: started with the rod horizontal ($\sin\varphi = 0$), the pendulum stays frozen while the solver reports success. A low state_priority on the momenta in derivative causality, @variables p(t) [state_priority = -10] in a custom element, steers MTK to a state that holds everywhere; the example shows how.

Causal conflicts. Two flow sources on one 1-junction both try to set the shared flow, a causal conflict. MTK reports it as a structurally singular system:

@named a = Sf(1.0)
@named b = Sf(2.0)
@named conflict = Junction1(a => :pout, b)
try
    mtkcompile(conflict)
catch err
    first(sprint(showerror, err), 120)
end
"ExtraEquationsSystemException: The system is unbalanced. There are 2 highest order derivative variables and 3 equations."

Signals

Activated bonds carry information but no power. Sources and two-ports without a value read it from input (a RealInput), and the detectors De and Df put an effort or a flow on output (a RealOutput), so MTK Standard Library blocks can drive and read a bond graph. See Signals and Detectors.
